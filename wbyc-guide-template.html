RANGEFINDER

Striving to be the best part of your day.

A GPS rangefinder built for one course — every hole, hazard, and green at White Bear Yacht Club, in your pocket and working offline. Here's the whole app in two screens.

1 · The Main Screen

Stand over your ball; the phone becomes the ball. Everything you need for the shot lives here.

  1. Tee selector. Tap your tee — the yardage line and the GPS‑OFF start point follow. Red & Green score against women's par.
  2. CENTER distance. The big number is yards to the middle of the green. Under it, "plays like" folds in elevation, wind, and your club (e.g. plays 152 · 7i).
  3. Map views. SAT aerial · GRN green zoom · PIN today's cup position · ★ pin a memory to a spot · 3D flyover with an animated tracer.
  4. Wind arrow. Points where the wind blows relative to the hole; the number is mph. Up = tailwind to the green.
  5. You. The glowing gold ball is your GPS position — walk and it walks with you. A pink "finding you…" marker shows until it locks on.
  6. Shot tracking. Set the club (7i = 7‑iron), tap SHOT at your ball. The app learns your real distances; long‑press to undo.
  7. Score strip. TOTAL SCORE and PUTTS for this hole — the whole width, thumb‑sized buttons, numbers you can read at arm's length in sun. Every tap saves itself.
  8. CARD. Up on the hole bar. Tap it to open the full scorecard (screen 2), where your running total and holes played live.
  9. Status card. Green dot + "GPS ±5m" = tracking you. WIND / ± ELEV toggle the "plays like" math; GPS ON/OFF switches live tracking vs. tap‑to‑place.
  10. Hazards & targets. Carry and reach distances to every bunker, water hazard, and dogleg on the hole.

2 · The Scorecard

Tap CARD ▸ to open your round — styled like the printed club card, with three views along the top.

  1. Three views. CARD is your hole‑by‑hole round; CLUB TRACKS shows your learned distance per club; STATS is your round history, trend line, and bests.
  2. Tap a hole to jump. Any hole row takes the app straight to that hole. Your current hole is highlighted.
  3. OUT / IN / TOTAL. Front nine, back nine, and total — strokes only, no depressing over‑par tally. Red/Green rounds score women's par automatically.
  4. POST to GHIN. Copies your hole‑by‑hole scores and opens ghin.com to paste — a deliberate two‑tap handoff, so your GHIN login never touches the app.
  5. SAVE ROUND. Watch its colour: pale while there's nothing to save, solid navy once a round is under way, and ringed in gold the moment you've entered nine or eighteen holes — that ring is the app telling you the round is ready to keep. This is how a round gets saved. It banks the finished card into your history — bests, averages and club distances all grow from it — then clears the card for next time. Your strokes are saved as you tap them, so NEW isn't "save my scores", it's "close this round out". If you forget and start playing again on another day, the app notices and offers to bank the old round for you.
  6. Export my data / Import my data. Back up your whole record to a file, or move it to a new phone. Rounds otherwise live only on the device that recorded them — no accounts, by design.

3 · What the App Remembers

Most of a golf season disappears. The card gets thrown away, and the shot you'll still be describing at Christmas leaves no trace at all. This part of the app exists to catch the bits worth keeping — some of it useful, some of it just yours.

★  Notes Pinned to Real Places

Tap the gold ★ above the map. It fills solid — ★ — and that's your cue it's armed; that's your cue it's armed. Then tap the spot on the hole you mean. The app asks what happened and pins your words to that exact place — not to the hole in general, to the spot. A star appears there, and it comes back every time you play the hole, for as long as you keep the app.

Tap it a second time if you change your mind; the gold drains away and nothing is pinned.

Two kinds of thing end up in there. The first is course knowledge that makes you play better next season: "never go left here" · "the 7‑iron is short when it's into the wind" · "bunker you can't see from the tee". Nobody remembers this from one August to the next, and it's precisely the local knowledge a member spends years acquiring. The second kind isn't useful at all: "drive of my life, 2 August" · "Dad's par from the trees". That's the point of it.

Tap any star on the map to read it again, or to remove it. Long stories are fine — the note sheet takes a good sentence or two.

The Line Above the Distances

Once you've banked a few rounds, a quiet italic line appears above the yardages on holes you've played before — birdied here, or last time: bogey. You never write it; it comes out of your own history, and it's the app's way of handing you the hole's record as you stand on the tee. Nothing to switch on.

{{DIARY}}

Open the scorecard and tap {{DIARY_BUTTON}}. The round you're playing is written up as a keepsake page: your score in plain English, the weather you actually played in, your birdies, your longest drive, and the moment the app judged to be the round's turning point. All of it comes from what the app already watched you do — you don't fill anything in.

Two things you can add yourself. Tap the note line to write a sentence about the day. And tap + add a photograph to put up to four pictures on the page, straight from your camera roll — the group on the first tee, the light coming across 9, whoever you were with. Photographs are resized on the way in so a whole season still fits on the phone; if storage ever runs tight the app says so plainly rather than losing one quietly.

Some rounds earn a seal — the club's bear, from the burgee, with something added. Wings for birdies, a club over the shoulder for the longest drive you hit, an umbrella for the weather you played in, and a plain gold one for going the full eighteen. You don't collect them and you can't chase them; they're just what the round happened to be.

Save or Print turns the page into something you can keep or hand to somebody. It's the one part of the app that isn't about the next shot.

All of it stays on your phone. Notes, photographs and rounds are stored on the device and nowhere else — there's no account, no server, and nobody at the club or anywhere else can read them. That also means they leave with the phone: use Export my data on the scorecard before you change handsets, and Import my data on the new one.

4 · Getting Started

Install It on Your Phone (~40 Seconds)

  1. Open wbyc-rangefinder.netlify.app in Safari (iPhone) or Chrome (Android).
  2. Tap Allow when it asks for your location.
  3. Tap the Share button → Add to Home Screen → Add.
  4. Launch it from the home‑screen icon after that — full screen, offline, screen stays awake during a round.

Your Name & Your Record

Tap + your name once (top right) — it signs your card and stays on the device. Then just play: every score and shot saves itself. At the last green, open the card and tap SAVE ROUND — that's the move that builds your trend, bests, and club distances.

Is the GPS working? Outdoors, the status card goes green and reads GPS ±5m, and the gold ball sits where you stand. Before it locks on you'll see a pink finding you… marker — normal. Always open the app from the web link (not an old saved copy), tap Allow for location, and be under open sky.

From then on FRONT / CENTER / BACK, "plays like", the carry rings and the 3D flyover all measure to the real cup instead of the middle: often a two‑club difference on a deep green. 6 · Good to Know

Pinch to Zoom

Two fingers zoom the hole map up to 8× and pan; pinch back out or change holes to snap back.

Ball Flight, Two Ways

The pink tracer shows on the hole map and animated over 3D terrain — with yardage stats at the apex.

Carry Arcs

Dashed rings show your selected club's reach (and its neighbors) from where you stand. They switch to your learned numbers after a few tracked shots.

Reading the Green

GRN shows a deep‑emerald green with white downhill arrows and 1‑ft contours — read the broad break, not 6‑inch putts.

Tournament‑Legal

Flip WIND and ± ELEV off for the raw, rules‑legal yardage (USGA Rule 4.3a). Leave both on for everyday golf.

Works Offline

Installed to your home screen, the app and your record survive with no signal. Live wind needs a connection.

A little whimsy hides in here for those who explore — long‑press ± ELEV to golf on the Moon, for one.
